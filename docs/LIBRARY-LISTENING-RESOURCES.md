# Library, listening and resource tracking

Implemented September 27, 2026. SQLite remains the local source of truth; no additional database service is required.

## Library and incomplete series

The sidebar's **Manage books & series** opens metadata, storage and series controls. EPUB covers are decoded locally into bounded JPEG thumbnails using Pillow, with embedded metadata removed. No remote cover lookup occurs. Refreshing an existing book reads its retained original and preserves manually edited title/author fields, source IDs, prose, profiles, scene directions and audio. Unsupported or absent artwork uses a text cover. Title and author can also be edited directly.

Original files, enhanced audio, simple listening audio and voice examples have separate byte counts. SQLite payload size is a logical per-book measure, not a separately allocated physical file. The overall storage count includes shared database files and retained backups. **Remove** hides a book or series and retains its data; **Removed items → Restore** reverses it. There is no permanent purge in this version.

Series use explicit numeric reading order, independent of the text in a book title. A supplied ninth volume can be assigned position 9 without having volumes 1–8. Missing/planned placeholders are optional records with no source text. Importing and assigning the actual book replaces the placeholder at that position. Earlier available volumes contribute evidence only through confirmed series character identity links. Later volumes and unavailable books are excluded from earlier-book interpretation. The app does not automatically establish that a collection or character profile is complete.

Changing a book's series removes its current identity links; retained observations and artifact history survive. Names alone do not merge characters. Review links in **Cast & voices** after discovery identifies the cast. Adding an earlier volume or changing linked evidence makes dependent profiles eligible for refinement while unrelated accepted work stays reusable.

## Series scheduling and recovery

**Open series** offers discovery, profiles, direction, or full analysis. Preview is local and shows supplied books, positions, known requests and estimated cost. Full analysis scans books independently, with concurrency capped at two, then interprets supplied books in reading order. Profiles and direction alone run in reading order. Missing placeholders are skipped.

Each book has a durable child run under a series run. Its request/token allowance is shared across discovery and later phases, rather than reset between phases. The dollar guard includes cumulative tracked analysis spend for that book. The combined ceiling is displayed in the preview. Unknown pricing requires selecting request/token limits explicitly. A plan fingerprint prevents starting a preview whose membership, models or other effective inputs have changed.

A failed or budget-limited book stops new scheduling; already active requests may finish and are accounted for. Cancellation propagates to child jobs and retains accepted outputs. Books stay reserved until their parent run ends so concurrent metadata/membership edits cannot change an active run. Restarting interrupts unfinished jobs; a fresh preview and resume reuses matching accepted work. The series run manifest is retained as a book-scoped artifact for each supplied book. There is no autonomous retry loop that can exhaust an allowance in the background.

## Independent simple listening

Simple mode renders the exact selected source passage using one narrator. Its identity includes provider, model, voice and source anchors. It does not use enhanced character directions, alter book annotations, or replace selected production takes. The existing reader supplies passage highlighting and reading position. Local device voices, Gemini and a configured Breeze server are available; enhanced mode remains selectable.

The panel and bottom reader controls share one Play/Pause state and playback speed. The footer names the selected narrator and links back to **Voice & listening settings**. Changing either speed selector updates the other and adjusts the active warmup/buffer without discarding saved takes or restarting the current passage.

With **device voices**, explicit Play starts a short warmup with a visible progress meter: aim for 10 seconds of listening at the selected speed, stopping after at most three passages. Once playback begins, a serialized queue aims for 45 listening seconds of contiguous saved audio, considering the remaining current clip and at most 12 future passages. It never crosses the chapter boundary. Actual saved durations drive progress; a passage-count cap or chapter end can leave the buffer below its time target. At 2.5×, 75 seconds of original audio supplies 30 seconds of listening, so speed changes affect how much the queue prepares. The player also locally preloads the next two available audio clips.

With device voices, **Prepare rest of chapter** is an explicit alternative when generation cannot keep up: it shows the number of passages from the selected position to the chapter end, saves them one at a time, and leaves playback stopped. Gemini preparation can incur charges for every uncached passage; the UI explains this scope but does not quote a dollar total or impose a narration allowance. This is a browser-coordinated queue of durable single-passage jobs, not a persistent autonomous chapter job. Completed takes survive a reload/restart; an explicit action is needed to prepare the remaining work again.

Cached takes replay without requiring a provider key. Pause, Stop, switching books or changing configuration invalidate queued playback/preparation; a request already in flight can finish and retain its take. An error stops further preparation and exposes **Retry preparation** while leaving completed audio usable. Read-only job polling has bounded retries; an uncertain paid POST is never automatically resent. Duplicate requests for the same active session/passage join its existing job, unless that job has been cancelled. Playback errors also stop lookahead; another explicit Play rechecks the local cache.

**Breeze** (a self-hosted server on the local network) uses the same per-passage path as device voices: warmup, rolling lookahead and explicit **Prepare rest of chapter**, one passage request at a time. It does not use chunked chapter jobs. Its session is pinned to the selected server voice's revision and seed from the last **Check connection**, so a voice changed on the server starts a new session while earlier takes stay saved; the browser also keys its remembered session on that revision. Saved Breeze takes replay after a restart without contacting the server. Only `cloned` server voices can narrate. Measured generation is roughly real time (see [voice research](RESEARCH-VOICE.md#breeze-tts-self-hosted)), so listening faster than 1× needs the chapter prepared ahead.

The device-voice buffer reduces avoidable gaps but cannot guarantee uninterrupted 2–2.5× listening if synthesis remains slower than consumption. Prepare the chapter first when needed. Word timing, background whole-book pre-generation, and a separate simple-mode audiobook ZIP remain unimplemented. See the [word-highlighting proposal](WORD-HIGHLIGHTING.md) for the alignment work.

### Chunked Gemini chapter listening

Implemented September 28, 2026. Gemini narration is limited by the Google project's quota, not by text size: the observed tier allowed 10 requests per minute, 10,000 input tokens per minute and 100 requests per day per model, and daily counts reset at midnight Pacific time ([rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)). One request per passage (median 54 characters, about 177 per chapter in a real library) could not finish a chapter in a day. Simple listening with Gemini therefore prepares chapters in large chunks through a server job.

- **Chunks.** Each request is an exact chapter slice from one passage start to a later passage end, including the original paragraph breaks between passages. Nothing is added or rewritten. The planner never crosses audio that already exists, prefers to end at a scene change, then a paragraph break, then a sentence end, and folds a short tail into the previous chunk instead of spending a request on it. Single-passage takes from earlier listening do not fragment requests: a chunk may run through them.
- **Size.** Gemini 3.8 TTS returns at most 16,384 output tokens per request. Measured responses report 32 audio tokens per second (not the 25 quoted in pricing material), so one request holds about 512 seconds of audio. Full chunks target 420 seconds (configurable 30–470) using the chunk's calibrated speech rate, and a conservative rate caps each chunk below 470 seconds. Output at the token cap, or 508 seconds or longer, is treated as truncated: it is not retained, the cap shrinks to 75% of the failed chunk, and at most two truncations are tolerated per job.
- **First audio.** Play uses quick-start steps (default 30 s, then 60 s, then full chunks) so listening begins within seconds. **Queue chapter** uses full chunks only. Presets and the full chunk length are in the listening panel; exact steps, requests at once (1–3, default 2) and the RPM/TPM/RPD limits for each speech model are under **Settings → Narration request limits**.
- **Pacing.** Every Gemini speech request (chapter chunks, single passages, voice examples, enhanced takes) passes one process-wide limiter with a 61-second sliding window for requests and estimated input tokens. A per-minute HTTP 429 pauses all senders for Google's retry delay and the rejected chunk is requested again, at most five consecutive times. A daily-quota 429, or this library's own count reaching the configured requests per day, stops the job as `quota_limited` with finished audio kept, and blocks that speech model in this server process until midnight Pacific: new chapter jobs are refused (HTTP 429) and paced requests fail without sending. Saving limits in Settings lifts the block (for example after a tier upgrade). **Resume chapter** after the reset continues. The daily count is recounted from the ledger before every send, rolls over at the reset and covers only this library, so it is a lower bound: another app or Bardic instance using the same Google project shares the quota. Rejected requests are counted, which is conservative. A paced single request (voice example, single passage, enhanced take) checks cancellation while it waits and fails rather than waiting more than two minutes.
- **Uncertain requests are never resent.** A read timeout or dropped connection may still have been processed and billed, so the job stops with its finished chunks kept. The read timeout scales with the chunk and the narrator's measured generation speed (at least 90 s + 1.5 × expected audio, at most 900 s). There is no narration dollar allowance.
- **Only explicit actions spend requests.** Play on a passage without audio starts or joins the job; automatic continuation at the end of a passage never does. After a job failed, was stopped or hit its quota, Play still plays saved audio but generation restarts only through **Resume chapter**. A new job inherits the narrator's learned speech rate and truncation ceiling, and until a full-size chunk has been measured it keeps only one full-size request in flight; simultaneous truncations planned from one estimate count once. If the coordinator itself fails, it waits for requests already sent before reporting. A Play that joins a job as it closes is either picked up or refused with a retryable 409, never silently dropped.
- **Listening while it works.** Play starts or joins the chapter job at the selected passage and begins as soon as that passage's chunk exists. Jumping ahead moves the job's focus there (and restarts quick-start steps if that passage has no audio); passages before the job's start extend its scope. When Play starts a job with at least 90 listening seconds already ready ahead, it uses full-size chunks instead of quick-start steps. **Stop** stops playback only; the chapter keeps generating until **Stop generating**, which lets requests already sent finish and keeps their audio. While another chapter of the book is generating, that chapter's saved audio still plays but Queue chapter is unavailable. One chapter job per book runs at a time, and it reserves the book like other jobs.
- **Progress and pace.** The panel shows passages ready from the listening position, listening time ready and still to generate at the current speed, an ETA, requests still needed and this library's requests today. It simulates the remaining chunks with the job's concurrency, per-minute limit, daily count and measured generation speed, and states whether playback at the current speed will stay ahead, when first audio is expected, how long to wait for gap-free listening, or which slower speed is safe now. Estimates start from priors (14 characters/s speech, 2× realtime generation) and recalibrate from finished chunks.
- **Reader marks.** Ready passages have a thin underline that alternates between two shades by chunk, with a small dot where a chunk starts. Passages being generated have an animated dotted underline and queued ones a faint dashed underline (no animation with reduced motion).
- **Playback and timing.** A chunk is one WAV, so playback is gapless within it; the player follows passages by clip offsets without reloading. Passage boundaries inside a chunk are **estimated** by aligning phrase-ending punctuation to pauses in the audio (`pause_alignment` version 1). On concatenated real Gemini takes with pauses trimmed to 25–60 ms, 85–100% of boundaries fell within 0.5 s (median 0.01–0.08 s). This is not word timing or speech recognition, and it cannot prove every word was spoken. Audio implausibly short for its text (more than 26 characters per second) is rejected like truncated output and never becomes passage audio. Weak pause matching is flagged `weak_alignment`. A chunk applies only while its chapter slice, passage anchors and texts, narrator session and recipe fingerprint all still match; a chunk whose WAV fails its content check is excluded from listing, playback and planning.

After a server restart the job is marked interrupted; **Resume chapter** starts a new job that reuses every finished chunk. Stopping the server waits for requests already sent.

`listening_sessions`, immutable `listening_takes` and immutable `listening_chunks` store source-bound metadata. The rebuildable `listening_synthesis_cache` index finds equivalent speech inputs across passages and books: exact text, provider/model, narrator voice and versioned audio recipe. Reuse validates the WAV and its actual SHA-256, retains a new source-bound take with an explicit pointer to the retained original take, and copies bytes into the target book's audio directory when needed. It preserves the original producer fingerprint and does not charge its original usage again. Existing exact-source cache hits lazily populate the index; there is no eager full-library scan on upgrade.

WAV files live under `listen-audio/<book-id>/` with content-addressed filenames. Copies across books save synthesis work rather than promising global disk deduplication. Analysis exports include session/take metadata; the existing audiobook export packages enhanced production audio.

## Contextual voice examples

**Hear example** appears beside the simple narrator, each cast Gemini/device/Breeze voice, and the Studio passage speaker. It uses the relevant passage, capped to an exact prefix of 400 Unicode code points; the panel labels shortened excerpts. A cast audition prefers the selected passage if assigned to that character, then a passage in the current chapter, then the first attributed passage in the book. A character with no assigned passage uses original demo text. Simple listening previews the selected/first chapter passage without cast directions; Studio auditions use that row's text and chosen speaker.

Cast examples include unsaved voice/direction choices, and Studio examples include unsaved passage speaker/direction choices. Other scene/cast inputs come from the saved book. No form is saved by auditioning; original prose and selected simple/enhanced takes are unchanged. Device voices ignore acting directions, as they do in ordinary narration.

One click explicitly requests one short sample. Matching previews reuse saved audio after content-hash/WAV verification; otherwise Gemini can incur charges, while Breeze samples run on the configured server without a per-request charge. A Breeze example retains the pinned voice revision and seed in its recipe. The app does not automatically generate samples when a dropdown changes, retry a generation POST or prepare later passages for an example. Examples use the bottom player's transport and current shared speed. Starting one pauses reading and stops its preparation queue while retaining the reader bookmark. If a listening request is still in flight, the example waits for that cancelled request to finish before submitting its sample. Returning to listening or preparing the rest of a chapter likewise waits for a cancelled example to settle before starting narration. Closing the example returns to that paused location; press Play to resume the book. Another selection or Stop invalidates pending sample playback even if the provider later finishes.

The independent version-1 archive stores `voice_preview_requests` and `voice_preview_takes`, plus WAVs under `voice-previews/<book-id>/`. Effective text/source/provider/model/voice/delivery inputs determine reuse within the book. These auditions are not selected production takes and do not populate the simple-listening cache. `voice_preview` resource operations record generation or cache reuse with the existing known/unknown usage rules. Preview bytes and database payloads count toward library storage. Keep them in full-library backups; analysis/audiobook ZIPs do not currently include their archive or WAVs. A narration dollar allowance and multi-take comparison interface remain future work.

## Playback diagnostics

**Settings → Troubleshooting → Download troubleshooting log** downloads the newest 5,000 local diagnostic events as JSON. Browser events cover preparation/request/poll/cache failures and media waiting/resumption/errors; server events correlate failed, stopped and rejected-submission listening jobs. Event timestamps and operational IDs connect the playback symptom to its saved job record. HTTP/media error codes and playback speed are recorded when available.

The dedicated `diagnostic_events` table is a rotating troubleshooting window, separate from immutable story/take provenance and resource accounting. Browser payloads use a strict field/event allowlist: no source prose, free-form messages, stack traces, URLs or API keys. Duplicate suppression and a global 120-client-event-per-minute persistence limit bound noisy clients. Recording failures must not interrupt narration. Earlier unrecorded toasts and pruned events cannot be reconstructed; the log is not a complete audit of every application action. [API contract](API.md#local-troubleshooting-diagnostics)

## Resource ledger

**Production studio → Resource usage** aggregates durable operations by stage and run, with a paginated operation list. Analysis attempts include retries and repairs. Narration tracks provider token reports where available, audio seconds and bytes. Import, local census, validation, publication, search, structure repair, metadata refresh and exports record local timings where instrumented. Cached work is labeled separately and does not inherit the historical charge of the original generation. Breeze narration records one request with estimated cost 0 and cost basis `self_hosted`: there is no per-request charge, but the server's GPU time is real compute that this ledger does not measure.

Elapsed time is the sum of measured operation durations, not total machine wall time; concurrent operations overlap. CPU time measures the current Python thread only, excluding `say`, ffmpeg, other subprocesses, GPU and provider compute. Old records and incomplete responses retain explicit unknown counts. Requests interrupted by restart are not silently reported as successful or free. HTTP success followed by unusable model output still counts as failure.

Costs are dated estimates computed from sufficiently complete provider usage. Missing usage or an unknown price does not mean zero cost. Gemini TTS estimates require the reported text/audio modality breakdown and cache counts needed by the rate calculation. The current research uses [Google's pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) and [Interactions usage fields](https://ai.google.dev/api/interactions-api); rate validity is retained with the estimate. Account-wide spending, balances, free-tier adjustments, discounts and invoice reconciliation are outside this ledger.

`resource_operations` stores operation status and measurements; existing `analysis_attempts` remain the source for analysis HTTP charges. The combined view avoids charging one request twice. `resource-operations.json` joins the portable analysis export; ledger rows contain no credential values or source prose. Polling the dashboard makes no model requests.
